# Merchant traffic in test mode

The sea-trial worker now enables the Layer 2.5 bridge described in [layer-2.5.md](layer-2.5.md). It adds knowledge-limited trade, physical report exchange, military role restrictions, cargo spoilage, wallet upkeep, diplomatic filtering, tactical hex threat and queue diversions. The following original Layer 2 notes describe the unchanged baseline engine and shared native sailing/docking components; global-price opportunity selection is retained only in that baseline, not the active bridge.

Global navigation plans major channel/port waypoints. `tack-helmsman.js` chooses only the next two upwind sailing legs, evaluating a fixed set of hull-specific angles, wind-driven speeds, turn costs and clearance. A conservative turning arc includes turning rate, forward velocity and hull yaw-response lag. Local legs refresh after a 0.1-radian wind shift, independently of the global 36-degree route threshold, and planning retries after interruptions are throttled to avoid per-frame searches. Existing debug routes combine upcoming local legs with the remaining major route and retain the actual dock tile as their final destination.

The deterministic helmsman prepares at full canvas when native `Ship.tackReady` is false, commits to one exit heading while crossing the wind, and resumes waypoint tracking after clearing the opposite no-go boundary and recovery margin. Failed entry or expired momentum is detected from native luffing and causes the ship to bear away and rebuild speed. Ordinary route updates wait until the committed turn finishes; urgent shoreline, traffic and threat avoidance can interrupt. There is no recurring tack timer or random tack choice.

AI sailing still runs the player's unchanged `Ship.step` at 60 Hz, with the same entry-speed threshold, momentum allowance, auto-trim, minimum steerage and sail increments. The helmsman outputs rudder and canvas only and never sets velocity, grants momentum or sets the physics tacking state. No trained policy or runtime physics rollouts are used.

Offline tuning with `node tests/tack-helmsman.test.mjs` covers seven hulls, wind strengths 0.1/0.5/1 and both incoming tacks: 42 deterministic open-water trials, 148 completed tacks, no luffing, groundings or substantial steering reversals. A separate forced momentum-loss case verifies that native luffing remains possible and the helmsman recovers. These trials validate open-water handling; generated-world and right-of-way suites separately check navigation, cargo, docking and traffic. Browser frame rate is not measured by these headless tests.

Voyages replan when local wind direction differs by more than 36 degrees (10 percent of a full circle) from the wind recorded for the current plan. Rechecks compare against that original angle, including across north, rather than against the preceding tick. Replanning is limited to one attempt per ship every three seconds; unsuccessful attempts retain the previous route. Departure uses the route selected during loading; recovery requests a fresh plan.

Navigation shares a cached coarse shore/shelf distance field. A* costs favor clearance from both banks and deep water, and waypoint compression retains the clearance cost rather than cutting straight across a safer channel route. Deep routes are attempted first; if no draft-safe path exists, the planner can fall back to any water with a strong shallow-water cost. Shore lookahead and waypoint shortcut validation run at 5 Hz, while rudder response, native tacking and land collision remain at 60 Hz. Close-pass events are recorded on every physics substep. Live steering looks ahead through two turning radii plus hull clearance and chooses earlier tacks/turns, preferring roomy headings before constrained ones. Shallow water continues to reduce native ship speed.

AI encounters use predicted closest approach and COLREG Rule 12: port tack gives way to starboard tack, and the windward vessel gives way on the same tack; an uncertain windward tack is treated conservatively by a port-tack vessel. Overtaking retains give-way priority under Rule 13. Avoidance selects water-safe heading changes, favors passing astern and reduces canvas when clearance is limited. Anchored, docked and assisted ships are obstacles. Player pose is sent to the worker five times per second so merchants also steer around the player; player helm controls remain manual. This is arcade avoidance, not a physical collision solver or a complete COLREG implementation. Rule reference: https://www.navcen.uscg.gov/navigation-rules-amalgamated

Every dock has two reserved berths on opposite sides of its pier. Incoming ships reserve before assisted approach; occupied or reserved berths count against capacity. A shared manager includes player and AI requests, assigns separate anchorage positions and promotes queued ships in arrival order. Approach and departure maneuvers at a dock are serialized. Departing ships retain their slot until their guided departure clears it; cancellation, sinking and test reset release reservations. Anchored ships are excluded from stuck recovery. Tooltips show berth, queue position and give-way state. Ports outside the small merchant trade subset are still available to the player's docking manager.

Idle merchants wait at anchor and reserve a berth only for cargo service. After unloading, they move off the dock before their berth is released to the next queued ship. Standby vessels do not join the docking queue until they accept a loading job. Anchor positions use exact hex centres starting at ring two, leaving one intervening hex clear, and expand outwards as nearby safe positions fill. A hull-width corridor extending outward along the dock direction is excluded from anchorage. Both player and merchant undocking target the centre of the single adjacent hex directly outward from the dock, continuing the harbor-to-dock direction. Merchants rotate continuously during guided travel, finishing aligned with the first planned sailing waypoint; the player finishes facing outward.

Destinations and final debug waypoints are the actual rendered dock tiles. Deep-water approach points are navigation aids only. Intermediate waypoints are accepted within two hex cells; larger ships may anticipate turns earlier. Arrival checks sweep the movement between updates and remember close passes during committed tacks, so a ship does not later backtrack to a missed waypoint. Advancing requires a water-safe segment to the next point. Local tack legs also retain close-pass events. Final dock waypoints remain governed by docking proximity. On arrival merchants animate alongside the dock, stop and transfer cargo at ten units per simulated second, then move to anchor while awaiting orders. Initial merchant launches move to anchor, select a job, then dock before loading.

Player ships approaching within two cells receive a docking prompt in the sea-trial panel. Accepting guides the hull alongside; Undock releases it for manual sailing. Hover tooltips include merchant docking/service state.

Ships underway that remain in the same hex cell for twenty seconds automatically enter animated offshore recovery. Docked ships and ships in winds below 0.025 normalized strength are exempt. Recover stuck AI manually starts the same movement for eligible ships stalled for at least five seconds. No recovery teleports the hull.

Click Test ship to launch the player and a temporary merchant simulation. The sea-trial Merchant traffic slider selects 0–100 merchants, starting at six. Releasing the slider resizes the live fleet without resetting voyages, markets or player docking reservations; its setting persists between trials. Sloops, schooners, brigantines and fluyts launch in separated, draft-safe water around up to 24 suitable ports. Farthest-first port selection covers the archipelago while retaining the player's base. Initial merchants balance faction populations and port registrations, choosing geographically separated faction ports. Only the first launch prefers the test base; there is no two-ship central cluster. The initial worker request includes player position, so merchants cannot spawn directly underneath the player. Launch candidates prefer lateral separation and use one-unit radius steps to keep nearby ships visible without overlapping their hulls. Every third launch prefers profitable voyages at least one quarter of the map diagonal away, falling back to nearer jobs if none are profitable and navigable. Other merchants select the best profitable route normally. Removing test ships releases their berths and returns onboard cargo to its source stockpile. If safe launch water is unavailable, the status reports the actual fleet size. Every merchant loads real stock, carries its manifest, and unloads at its destination through Layer 1. Routes, controls, collision, tacking and local wind use Layer 2 and the existing Ship physics. A failed coastal replan retains the prior route, allowing the ship to pivot away from land rather than losing its steering target.

The sea-trial panel reports total merchants and the count moving. Fit view (button or 0) disables Follow ship, allowing the player to pan and zoom around traffic. Small maps without two compatible ports explain why no traffic can launch. Test mode uses explicit static rules: European factions trade with one another; Pirates trade with Pirates. This is workshop configuration, not the future dynamic faction engine. Merchants do not attack and the player has no cargo-trading interface yet.

Hover over a hull during test mode to inspect its cargo quantities, registration port, voyage origin and destination. Registration remains the original spawn port across later voyages; origin changes with each departure. Player hulls show an empty hold and manual destination because player cargo trading is not implemented. The sea-trial Show planned routes (debug) checkbox displays each merchant's remaining authoritative waypoints in its faction pennant color, including tack legs. Route geometry is isolated from sprite batching and clears on disable/exit.

`merchant-session.js` selects safe deep-water approach points near rendered docks and configures temporary markets through `createPortEconomy`. All generated ports have separate consumption reserves and trade supplies, even when they are outside the merchant navigation subset. Port specialties begin with surplus trade stock; other trade stocks begin scarce so meaningful routes exist immediately. Merchants cannot purchase consumption reserves. Raw production follows generated cotton, iron and sugar industries and enters trade stock; consumption reduces only the consumption pool. The governor's port-register controls move units between the pools without creating goods or taxes. Finished goods are preloaded test stocks; no manufacturing recipes are implied. Merchant wallets start at 20,000 gold. Every ten simulated seconds, production/consumption, prices and taxes advance before the logistics tick. Port supply inspection refreshes once per simulated second and after allocations. All state resets on exit or map regeneration.

`merchant.worker.js` runs physics at 30 ticks per second with 1/60-second substeps, publishing timestamped snapshots at 10 Hz. Merchant opportunity searches and global replans use a round-robin queue with a four-millisecond planning budget per worker tick. A* exploration and path compression yield between small chunks; a chunk may briefly exceed the budget. Ships continue sailing the last valid route while planning. Economic upper bounds avoid searching routes that cannot improve the current job, and short-lived port-route caches share geometry without sharing mutable waypoint arrays. The shore/shelf clearance field is prepared before the first snapshot; grid-edge safety and clearance costs are cached separately from wind-dependent drive.

The render thread buffers up to eight snapshots, interpolates at a nominal 100-ms delay and permits at most 200 ms of velocity prediction for late packets. Prediction changes presentation only and excludes moored and anchored ships. This replaces the fixed 100-ms animation that stopped at every delayed worker update. Merchant wakes use 48 history points, alternate segments, fewer strokes and 10-Hz rebuilding; player wakes retain their original detail. Wake geometry is skipped when its zoom-dependent alpha is zero, and merchant pennants are omitted at distant map zoom. Offscreen merchant geometry is culled. Shadows follow depth without additional blur filters per merchant. Prevailing wind and the existing local wind field are copied to the worker twice per second; no GPU readback or renderer-buffer transfer occurs.

Water and cloud filters adjust resolution after sustained low frame rates. Water ranges from 1.0 to 0.65 and cloud resolution follows at half that value; ship sprites and interface resolution stay unchanged. Two seconds of sustained overload lowers shader quality, while eight seconds of stable headroom permits gradual recovery. A single loading spike does not trigger a quality change. These measures reduce CPU/GPU work; browser FPS still depends on device and viewport.

Stopping the trial, regenerating the map or leaving the page terminates the worker and destroys merchant graphics. Changing the player's hull or resetting its launch retains the existing merchant session. Hidden tabs pause the worker. Session generations and pending asset slots reject stale worker/asset results, and failed sprite requests wait for a new test session before retrying.

Run `node tests/navigation-traffic.test.mjs` for wind thresholds, channel costs, shallow fallback, sailing right-of-way and berth/anchor queues. `node tests/waypoints.test.mjs` covers swept passes, committed tack arrival retention, safe shortcuts and final destination preservation. `node tests/docking.test.mjs` covers player prompts, animated anchorage/docking/departure and stale slot grants. `node tests/merchant-traffic.test.mjs` covers generated-world port spread and distant cargo routes, initial player exclusion, visible local merchants and whole-fleet fit view, timestamped updates with the local wind field, native Pixi geometry, worker ticking, live density changes, cargo conservation and shared player reservations. `node tests/traffic-performance.test.mjs` covers irregular snapshot intervals, bounded prediction, moored hulls, heading wrap, adaptive-quality hysteresis and yielding navigation. The existing logistics, economy and sailing-control suites cover accounting and movement. Browser frame rate has not been measured; headless worker timings are not GPU FPS measurements.


Faction warships resize with merchant density. Each represented faction has at least one frigate while traffic is enabled, plus the per-faction quotas: one frigate per five merchants, one war galleon per ten, and one man-of-war per fifteen (whole completed groups, cumulative tiers). These patrols are additional to the merchant slider count. Zero density removes both fleets. Military vessels remain non-trading and sail home-to-friendly-port / friendly-port-to-home legs. Military home selection fills the least-used owned ports first and prefers geographically separated assignments; friendly-route proximity and boundary clearance break remaining ties; inaccessible destinations are skipped, and hull-specific open-water approaches allow turning room.

Patrol departures plan while holding position instead of moving into tight anchor spaces first. Compatible hull/faction/home/destination routes share cooperative search results, with a fresh swept entry check for every vessel. Cached corridors expire after 120 simulated seconds or a wind shift above ten degrees; ships still use the normal live wind/threat replanning and native steering. Civilian and military searches alternate within the existing planning budget. The hex planner uses wind-aware weighted A* (weight 2.5), trading strict shortest-cost optimality for bounded practical search work. A verified clear, roomy, threat-free water corridor can bypass search. All hull clearance, shallow-water movement and coastal safety checks still apply.

The search budget counts actual expansions rather than discarded stale heap entries. Shared military searches may expand up to the map tile count so valid long detours are not cut off by the old 16,000-node cap; wall-time work remains cooperative within the existing per-tick planning budget. The Recover stuck AI action includes military vessels.

Spawn distribution is deterministic and runs only when adding ships. Both merchant and naval placements still check draft, hull clearance, player/ship spacing and safe water near the selected home port. Increasing density fills underused home ports without relocating existing ships or resetting their voyages.

Ports with an outward undocking tile that cannot safely accommodate the reference merchant hull are excluded from test fleet home assignments. This prevents peripheral docks from trapping newly distributed ships against the map boundary. Broad fleet distribution increases the number of distinct cold route searches, so full-fleet departure validation allows up to six simulated minutes while searches remain within the existing per-tick planning budget.

Within each civilian/military planning turn, ships waiting for their first departure are served before ships already sailing request another route. This keeps remote initial spawns from being starved by continuous fleet replanning without increasing the planning time budget or changing ship physics.

Weighted hex A* now closes each expanded tile once. This prevents repeated expansions from consuming the map-sized search allowance and incorrectly rejecting valid routes from distant faction outposts. Each route edge still uses hull clearance, wind and threat costs.
