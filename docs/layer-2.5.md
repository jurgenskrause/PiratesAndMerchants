# Layer 2.5 bridge

`bridge-logistics.js` owns ShipAgent orchestration and imports unchanged Layer 1 transactions and Layer 2 sailing, tacking, navigation, traffic avoidance and docking primitives. The private-agent Layer 2 engine remains intact as the baseline implementation. `createMerchantSession(..., {bridge:true})` selects the bridge; the sea-trial worker enables it. No core economic, logistics, ship physics or navigation source is modified.

## Roles and public interfaces

`VESSEL_ROLES` marks Sloop, Schooner, Brigantine and Fluyt as traders. Frigate, War Galleon and FIRST_RATE are military-only. They never run opportunity scans and `loadCargo` returns `MILITARY_ONLY`; no military sale path is permitted. `orderVisit` supplies a noncommercial navigation/docking order for either role. The trial spawns up to one military patrol per faction in draft-safe water; the traffic slider controls civilians separately. Each military agent has a fixed home port and alternates home → same-faction/allied port → home using native sailing, tacking and docking. Away-to-away and neutral/hostile destinations are rejected. If no allied destination exists, it waits at anchor. A broken alliance redirects an away-bound patrol home. All docking roles carry information. Future operations can supply external vessels, alter diplomacy and issue visits without adding merchant behavior to military classes.

## Knowledge and trade decisions

Each bridge Port and ShipAgent owns a `PriceLedger`. Launching agents start with a local commissioning report, never remote omniscient prices. Ports initially know only their own market. At docking, exact local prices, available trade units and tax are observed at `CurrentTick`. Newer remote entries upload to the port and download to the carrier. Original observation time and originating port are preserved; relaying does not make a report younger. Duplicate timestamps retain the existing report. Reports are copied and expire at the configured `ledgerMaxAge` (default 1,800 ticks).

An arbitrage matrix contains known buy/sell prices, cargo quantity, confidence, age, route risk and expected profit. Sell confidence is `max(0, 1 - age / maxAge)` and discounts the positive quoted resale margin toward the acquisition cost, rather than discounting the entire resale price. Known loss-making quotes are never raised by this adjustment. Purchase and sale taxes are included. Journey cost includes distance, estimated maintenance and faction-relative path risk, including cargo exposure. Hostile or embargoed destinations are excluded using current diplomacy. Only physical loading/unloading reads live market transactions. Unknown ports are eligible for empty information voyages, not invented profitable trades; repeated visits create knowledge and subsequent trading jobs.

Idle merchants review work every 5–6.2 simulated seconds, staggered by vessel ID. A cheap ledger-margin preflight ranks destinations before Hex A* work; route risk, taxes and journey costs must still leave at least the configured minimum profit. Pending local loading orders reduce the stock available to competing job estimates, encouraging cargo diversity without changing Layer 1 transactions.

When no viable cargo job exists, merchants can seek known export supply at another port, discover an unknown market or refresh a known report older than 30 bridge ticks. Market selection accounts for distance and other ships already visiting or surveying that port. Information and procurement positioning voyages sail empty and must be affordable; they do not fabricate cargo or revenue. Live market knowledge still arrives through physical docking. Completely fresh, unpromising markets may still justify a short wait. Tooltips identify Trade cargo, Seek cargo, Discover market, Refresh market report or Seeking work. Military role restrictions remain unchanged.

Initial anchorage failure leaves a merchant eligible for job scans. A failed loading attempt returns to the idle/anchoring flow, even when the port departure lane is temporarily busy; it cannot leave a permanent loading state with no order.

The player retains a worker-owned ledger across voyages and hull changes within the same trial. Docking exchanges it with any map port, including ports outside the merchant subset. View Ledger appears while docked and displays observations, ages, confidence, source and available units. Amber/red sections flag aging/stale reports. No remote-price preview populates the ledger.

## Clock and running costs

Bridge ticks are one simulated second, independent of the 30-Hz logistics update and the unchanged ten-second economic tick. Ledger timestamps and threat decay use this clock. Cargo has no spoilage in any state, including mooring, transit and anchorage. Commodity catalogs and route forecasts no longer define decay rates. Ship running costs accrue from elapsed sea time into funded purses and settle at the destination; docked handling adds no sea-time costs.

Daily maintenance is 2/4/6/10/18/25/40 gold for the seven classes, prorated each second using `secondsPerDay` (default 86,400). Waiting vessels pay the same upkeep. Deductions are capped at available wallet funds; debt is not introduced. Rates, day length, minimum viable profit (default 1) and queue review interval (default 15 seconds) are development configuration.

## Tactical hex threat

`ThreatGrid` uses pointy-top axial/cube cells and exact cube distance. Only active military-class or Pirate-faction vessels emit. A single emitter deposits 100%, 75%, 50%, 25% strength over rings 0–3 (1/6/12/18 cells). Concurrent emitters add up to `MaxThreat` (default 100) independently per faction. Historical values decay linearly by `DecayRate` (default 2) each tick; current deposits refresh the field. Stationary single emitters preserve their ring falloff rather than accumulating every ring to maximum. Each cell also tracks per-faction last-patrol tick and frequency score. Frequency follows an EMA with a 120-tick half-life and receives a weighted impulse only on vessel entry into a new hex. Stationary vessels refresh local recency but never accumulate frequency. Friendly coverage combines recency and frequency; repeated visits extend the recency window from 30 ticks, capped at 500, while frequency suppression is capped at 85%. Effective hostile threat is base threat × (1 − recency factor) × (1 − frequency factor). At 500 ticks without a visit, protection expires regardless of past frequency. Obsolete metadata is pruned once its frequency falls below 0.01.

Civilian navigation uses hostile base values suppressed by same-faction/allied patrol coverage. Neutral and hostile patrols provide no protection. A TacticalNavigator extends the unchanged Navigator, adds sampled threat costs to segments and prevents the open-water direct-route shortcut from bypassing hostile cells. Compression retains threat costs. Route planning is cooperative under a shared four-millisecond budget, with native physics continuing between planning chunks. Ship sailing still uses native `Ship.step` at 60-Hz substeps. Global wind shifts and threatening routes trigger bounded replans.

F1/F2/F3/F4 toggle hostile-threat views for England/France/Spain/Pirates. Visible sparse hexes render residual threat in yellow/red and friendly patrol coverage in blue where residual threat is negligible. Buttons provide the same controls. Diplomacy (D) shows standings, war declarations, alliances and embargoes from the actual relation matrix. The Diplomacy API supports symmetric updates; this screen is read-only and automatic diplomacy simulation belongs to Layer 3.

## Queue diversion

Queued merchants review ledger profit and cumulative waiting maintenance every 15 seconds. Unviable, expired or diplomatically invalid orders release their berth/queue reservation immediately, before asynchronous alternative planning. Existing cargo is retained without decay. They seek a permitted alternative using ledger information and tactical navigation. A safe information/diversion port is used when a viable arbitrage opportunity is unavailable; if there is no reachable permitted port, the vessel remains anchored without reserving a berth and retries. Arrivals reserve one of the normal two berths and perform timed cargo service. Undocking still ends at the adjacent outward hex and rotates towards the first route waypoint.

## Observation controls

The governor panel is removed. Each map port retains an independent governor identity and prosperity state in the bridge; detailed governor policies can be added later without a player allocation panel. Existing economic reserve management remains unchanged.

Map settings and ship settings are native collapsible disclosures. The legend area has a 1–20× target simulation-speed slider and achieved speed readout. The worker advances additional unchanged 1/30-second logistics steps, with native 1/60-second ship substeps; economy, maintenance, queues, ledgers and threat share accelerated simulated time. A 24 ms callback work budget lowers the achieved rate under load instead of increasing physics dt. Rendering/interpolation and player sailing use the achieved rate. Hidden tabs remain paused.

Faction icons appear in ship tooltips. Clicking a ship without dragging pins its tooltip at the right. More info fetches only that ship’s ledger and voyage records, refreshed each simulated second. Active ships opens a sortable table for realised profit/hour, total profit, cargo, capacity, hull maximum speed, faction, class and status; rows select and locate ships. Dialog keys are isolated from sailing controls.

Voyage journals record actual purchase and sale transactions, loaded/delivered quantities, source/destination, start/end ticks and prorated upkeep. Realised profit equals sales minus purchases minus voyage upkeep. Unsold cargo retains its purchase cost without inventing sale proceeds. Reroutes preserve costs and update the actual destination. Journals retain the latest 100 records plus lifetime totals; in-progress profits are not counted as completed earnings. Information/patrol voyages record empty holds and upkeep. Player voyages record dock visits and current ledger, with no invented trade transactions. Military/player profitability columns are N/A.

## Uniform simulation optimizations

All ships retain the same 30-Hz orchestration and native 60-Hz physics. No distance-based update tiers, skipped physics or coarse sailing mode are used. Tactical navigation samples only the depth required for exactly the same seven hull probes and half-unit segment spacing, avoiding full terrain inspection objects. Bounded static geometry/cost caches are shared by faction navigators. Faction-relative threat metrics and conservative hazard bounds are memoized for each threat tick and diplomacy revision; paths outside all hostile hex bounds skip unnecessary samples. New patrol ticks and diplomatic changes invalidate these caches immediately. Traffic snapshots contain only the fields needed by the unchanged avoidance rules, and each agent reuses its shallow sailing specification.

`tests/simulation-optimization.test.mjs` compares 10,000 depth lookups, 1,400 native geometry/cost checks and 500 sampled route-risk paths exactly, and covers threat/diplomacy cache invalidation. The generated-map profile is a headless benchmark, not a guarantee of browser FPS or achieved acceleration.

## Sailing tiers

Every moving bridge ShipAgent, civilian or military, uses the same deterministic macro/micro steering pipeline. Macro routing is cooperative A* on the actual pointy-top hex grid (six neighbors, bounded node exploration and binary heap). It captures global prevailing wind at creation, uses each hull’s polar efficiency to penalize upwind traversal by estimated VMG, adds faction-relative threat cost, and retains shore/channel clearance preference. Waypoints remain hex endpoints; bounded visibility compression keeps wind, threat and clearance costs. Local wind variations are handled by steering rather than baked into a global route. The existing exact destination dock and shallow-water fallback remain in place.

RouteHelmsman steers directly toward crosswind/downwind waypoints and uses land-checked optimum port/starboard sailing legs when the waypoint lies in the hull’s no-go cone. Corridor width adapts to speed and turn radius so large hulls retain maneuvering room. Reaching a local leg/corridor boundary switches tack. Three forward probes at 0° and ±30° are refreshed every 0.2 simulated seconds for every vessel; distance scales with velocity and turn room. Shoreward motion requests the safe opposite tack, with the same native entry-speed threshold and momentum physics. If both vectors are blocked, the ship requests immediate macro replanning and steers toward available open water while it is computed. Candidate counts remain bounded; no rollout simulation, learning or intent prediction is used.

A global direction deviation strictly over 10° must persist continuously for more than five simulated seconds before wind-triggered replanning. Returning inside the threshold resets the timer, and angle wrap is handled. New faction-relative threat hotspots strictly over 50/100 on the remaining corridor trigger immediately, without the wind delay. Existing unchanged hotspots do not cause repeated replans; cooling and regenerating hotspots can trigger again. Failed route searches retry with a short backoff; duplicate pending jobs are avoided. Successful routes reset the monitored wind baseline to the captured creation wind. Native physics and the baseline Layer 1/2 engines remain unchanged.

`tests/sailing-route.test.mjs` covers hex routes, polar penalties, direct sailing, forced coastal tacks, blocked fallback, direction snapshots, hysteresis, threshold/wrap edges, off-corridor/low-threat exclusions and immediate high-threat replanning. `tests/route-sailing.test.mjs` exercises 42 native 60-Hz trials across seven hulls, three wind strengths and both initial tacks.
