# Phase 2.2: Aggregated feeder roads

Industry spawning now builds feeder roads on the generation worker. `findFeederRoad` uses deterministic hex A* with a binary heap, integer tenths for cost comparisons, and an admissible minimum-distance-to-goal heuristic. Normal land costs 1.0; connected existing roads cost 0.1. Its goals are the owning port's town/core and connected road tiles within feeder range. Industry endpoints are excluded from other industries' road goals, so branches join the trunk instead of passing through production sites.

The default configurable feeder radius is 18 hexes. Heights at or above 0.78 are mountain barriers; all water, off-map cells and foreign town/core/dock land are blocked. All rock/stone biome tiles are blocked regardless of elevation. Rocky mine sites remain industry tiles: their road stops at the edge. Road cells become biome 6 (Road), with map marker 5, replacing their former terrain type and appearance. Original terrain is retained separately only for deterministic regeneration.

Searches remain inside twice the feeder radius around the core and expand at most 4,096 nodes. Inaccessible or budget-limited routes leave the network unchanged and record a reason. No road pathfinding runs in the animation loop or ordinary sailing ticks. New industry placement can call `connectFeederIndustry` incrementally; generation first floods eligible land around each port, then confirms an A* connection before committing each industry. Unserved islands, out-of-range sites and unreachable candidates spawn no industry. A deterministic rebuild clears roads when industry density becomes zero.

Connections retain explicit reciprocal edge masks. Creating a branch updates its predecessor and the joining trunk tile immediately. Merely placing road cells beside each other does not invent a parallel-road connection. The existing minimal tile set resolves all straight, curve and three-way masks; busier junctions retain their full logical connectivity with the existing visual fallback. Roads use an opaque painted full-hex atlas, with transparent space outside each hex and nine prerendered base frames. Road graphics remain fully opaque; buildings retain their zoom fade. Core road graphics lie beneath the port office. Large islands may get up to three spaced, same-faction ports, configured through the settlement registry; additional ports are optional, not guaranteed.

JSON settings: `feederRoadRadius`, `roadMountainHeight`, `roadMaxSearchNodes`. The existing catalog compilation/versioning workflow includes these values, preserving the repaired zero-fetch registry startup.

Verification includes shared branches, junction masks, reciprocal edges, deterministic rebuilding, isolated-road exclusion, mountain detours, rocky mine access, water-separated islands, failed-route atomicity, range/search limits, foreign settlements, generated 256-square worlds, and industry-density clearing. Existing industry, registry, dock and startup checks remain covered.

## Road artwork

Asset: `dist/gfx/roads/painted-atlas-v1.png`. Generated with the built-in image tool in generation mode, then assigned atlas frames in the asset registry. Prompt: a transparent 3×3 atlas of standalone pointy-top hex watercolor road tiles, opaque earthy ground, tan dirt paths with grass verges and wheel ruts, matching the existing painted top-down game. Frames: endpoint NW, straight NW–SE, gentle NW–E curve, sharp NW–NE curve, Y NW/E/SW, T NW/NE/SE, fork NW/NE/E, offset junction NW/E/SE, urban infill. Exact edge connectivity remains governed by the road bitmask resolver.

## Zoom levels

Road tiles use filtered mipmaps and a cached overview texture per logical connection mask. The overview is an opaque, quiet full hex with simple connecting strokes, rasterized once at 128×128 pixels. It keeps every logical branch, including junctions beyond the painted subset. Painted artwork fades over it between 12 and 36 screen pixels per hex. At 3–7 pixels per hex the overview itself fades into the terrain shader's road-colored ground. Buildings fade between 12 and 30 pixels per hex. All transitions use smoothstep; ordinary frame updates only change alpha, visibility and camera transforms. Offscreen groups are culled, and map rebuilds reuse the texture cache. Overview render textures have mipmap levels allocated before drawing, preserving their contents; integer square bake bounds avoid Pixi's integer frame rounding clipping hex edges.
